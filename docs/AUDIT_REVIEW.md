# Account audit (the Instant Audit): the money test, and a review of the old screen (5 Nov 2026)

King's words: "customer first, money first: is this sellable, does it resolve a pain point, will customers buy; sexy and lovable, NOT overwhelming; works for agencies AND client-account users and on a phone; add the features that are missing."

## Part 1. The money-first test (in plain words)

**1. The pain.** An owner (or an agency running 20 or 100 clients) cannot see what is leaking. People who wrote in and were never answered, appointments that nobody marked, no-shows that nobody called back, deals that stopped moving months ago: each of these is invisible until a client complains or a month's numbers look bad. The CRM keeps every record and shows none of this as one picture. The owner finds out by accident.

**2. Who feels it.** The **business owner** (money walks out and they do not know how much). The **agency owner** (their retainer is judged on results they cannot see across clients; they lose a client and only then find 30 unanswered messages). The **setter** (does not know which conversations to start with). The **agency's salesperson**, who needs one thing to show a prospect in five seconds.

**3. The five-second proof.** The first thing on the screen is one sentence: **"Your client accounts are leaking about $299,572 a month."** with the word *Estimate* above it and "How this is worked out" under it. Below it, one button: **"Review the 23 deals"** (or "Reply to the 96 people"), and the findings in plain words: *96 people are waiting for a reply. 23 open deals worth $1.2M have gone quiet. 8 no-shows have not been rebooked.* (Those are the demo company's own numbers; a real account sees its own.) This is the "we found 23 leads nobody answered, 9 no-shows never followed up, 14 stale deals worth about $X (estimate)" moment, with the count on every button equal to the rows behind it.

**4. Why would anyone pay for this, when the CRM has its own reports and free audit tools exist? (said honestly)**
- The CRM's reports are **per account and per thing**: an appointments report here, a pipeline report there. None says "this is what is wrong and what it costs", none pushes the finding at you, and none works across 40 clients.
- Free "audit" tools (marketing-site graders, spreadsheet templates) look at ads and websites, not at the inside of the CRM where the sales actually leak.
- What is sellable: **speed to value** (it runs the minute SubSync has read the account, no setup), **one number a non-technical owner understands**, **an honest list of exactly who and which deals**, and **a link or a printout a salesperson can hand a prospect**. The audit is the shop window. The thing people pay for monthly is the alerts and queues around it (speed to lead, Today), which tell them the same thing every morning.
- Honest verdict: **the audit alone is not what keeps a customer; it is what gets the first yes.** It earns its place as the onboarding hook and the marketing asset (the shareable page), and as the free-tier front door to the paid features. A buyer will not pay monthly for a report they read once; they will pay for the part that watches.
- The risk to trust is the dollar number. A figure that is too big or repeated five times reads as a gimmick. So the rebuild says one number once, calls it an estimate, puts its formula one tap away, refuses to invent a figure where an account has too few closed deals, and keeps counted money (deals) apart from estimated money (people), never adding them.

**5. What to cut.** Cut in the rebuild: the four-tile stat row that repeated the same dollar figure (the sentence says it once); the "Good to know" label that was stuck on real problems; "Where to start" as a ten-row table that stopped at ten; the finding "thin contact records" (not money, lives in Accounts); the finding "1 account needs the calendar permission" (now one line under the findings, because it is a setting, not a leak); the finding "N open deals are left out of every dollar figure" (replaced by one finding about old and test deals that names what to do); the "13 accounts left out by the filter" line (it also told a client's staff how many other accounts exist). Not built on purpose: prospect mode (running the audit on someone who has not installed needs their login; the shared link already lets a salesperson hand over the result), automatic sending of anything.

## Part 2. The old screen, reviewed

Photographed on the demo company at 1440, 768 and 390 for the four roles (agency all accounts, agency inside one account `?as=location`, a client's staff `?as=client`, a business owner `?as=business`): `docs/review/audit-old-<role>-<width>.png`. The new screen is photographed the same way: `docs/review/audit-<role>-<width>.png`.

**What is confusing, repeated or untrustworthy (each point seen on the screenshots or in the code):**

1. **One dollar figure, five times.** On the agency view `$1,397,934` appears in the subtitle, in "What to fix first", as the big stat, in the first finding's title and again in the "Where to start" table's rows. The estimate (`$156,519`) appears in the stat, in the second finding ("up to $156,519") and per client. A reader cannot tell whether that is five problems or one. This is exactly the repetition CLAUDE.md 13A warns about.
2. **One deal is 72% of the number.** `$1,000,000` is a single deal. The old page does say so ("check that it is a real deal"), but in small text under the figure; the headline sentence still reads as a pattern across 13 accounts. The rebuild keeps the warning and puts it with the number.
3. **The same deal and the same person are in several findings.** A person waiting for a reply and a quiet deal on the same contact, or a no-show who is also waiting, were counted in each. (Today fixed this for people waiting; the audit did not.) Nothing said what overlapped.
4. **One global "stale" age.** Every deal untouched for 30 days is "stale" whether it is a three-day sales cycle or a long one, and a test deal from last year counts the same as a live one. The page had no way to separate a real quiet deal from a leftover. (Per-stage ages need stage history, which is still being collected; the rebuild instead separates deals that went quiet recently from deals untouched for more than three times as long, or named like a test, exactly as Today does.)
5. **Two tiles, two vocabularies.** "Deals that stopped moving", "Cost of slow follow-up", "stale deal", "open pipeline", "left out of every dollar figure", "thin contact records": six terms for what a person wants as one sentence.
6. **"Good to know" on problems.** Eight past appointments with no outcome, 2 no-shows, 67 open deals left out: each was tagged "Good to know" because the severity was "info", which reads as reassurance. Dot-and-pill status tags in blue, orange and red are the style King rejected (KF-1).
7. **Three buttons for one list.** "Show the 43 deals" appeared in "What to fix first", under the stat as "Show the 43 deals behind this", and in the finding row, beside "Open in Pipeline and deals", which leaves the page for a different screen with the list unfiltered. The one action per finding was not clear.
8. **A row is a wall.** Each finding was about 150 px tall with a title, a sentence, a "Counted over" line, a "What to do" line, three labels and two buttons. At 390 px the page is 4,844 px long.
9. **A win rate on a tiny sample is shown as a dollar estimate with a range**, which is correct under 13A (needs 20 closed deals) but when it was missing the tile said only "Not enough data" without saying which accounts, so an agency could not tell if one client had a figure and the others did not. Now: "N people in accounts with under 20 won or lost deals are not priced".
10. **Clients: only the top 10 and no way to see the rest.** "Showing 10 of 10" while the agency has 13. No search, no sort, no paging, nothing server side. At 100 accounts this is a table of 100 rows or a silent cut.
11. **A client's staff and a business owner were told how many other accounts exist.** "13 client accounts are left out by the filter at the top of the app" is shown to a user who must never learn about other accounts. Fixed: the number is never sent to an account user.
12. **No first-run state.** Right after install, the audit shows 0 and "Nothing urgent" until the first read finishes, which a buyer reads as "my account is perfect". There is no "reading your accounts: 5 of 12" message and no automatic refresh.
13. **No good news and no progress.** The page only ever lists problems. A customer who fixed 20 things sees the same page next week with no sign that anything improved.
14. **No "do this first".** There was a "What to fix first" line, chosen by severity labels, not by money, and it repeated the first finding. It did not say why that one.
15. **No run again.** The page was "checked just now from the latest sync" with no way to read again.
16. **Phone.** Buttons are small text-like pills below long paragraphs; "Show the 43 deals" is about 32 px high.

**What the rebuild does about each point:** (1) the sentence says the money once, with "Estimate" above it; counted deal money is said once in a separate line; (2) the outlier warning sits with the number; (3) the counting rule (a person or a deal is counted once; deals first, then replies, then no-shows, then appointments) is in `docs/METRICS.md` and unit tested; (4) deals that went quiet recently are separated from old or test deals, which are their own finding and never in the money; (5) four findings in plain words; (6) no tags, status words are plain text, and the grade is "N of 4 checks are clean"; (7) each finding has ONE button, which opens exactly those rows in the shared list; (8) a row is a title, a sentence, a money line, one fix line and a formula one tap away; (9) people in accounts under 20 closed deals are counted but not priced, and the page says how many; (10) top five, then "See all N clients" with search, order and paging on the server (tested at 150 accounts); (11) no hidden-account count for an account user (tested); (12) "Reading your client accounts: 5 of 12 done" with a progress bar that refreshes itself every 20 seconds; (13) a "What is healthy" block and a "Progress" block with minimum-sample rules; (14) one calm primary button, named after the action ("Reply to the 96 people"); (15) "Run the audit again"; (16) 44 px buttons at 390 px, tested.
