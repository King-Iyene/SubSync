## 2. The five evaluation lines (principle 1)

- **(a) Decision and for whom.** An owner or ops person decides what they want to be told, where (bell, email, a Slack channel per team) and when (a morning brief, a Monday summary, quiet at night). A setter wants one ping when a lead is waiting. The agency owner decides whether a client's own Slack may receive that client's alerts.
- **(b) Is the data truly available, tested not assumed.** Our own data: yes (alerts, the digest, the sync alarm are existing, tested sources). Slack: the request shapes follow Slack's documented incoming-webhook sign-in and its documented plain-text webhook answers, tested against a fake Slack only: **NOT VERIFIED LIVE** (the redirect address is not saved in the Slack app yet, N92). Email: the sender and its answers are the existing Resend call; **NOT VERIFIED LIVE** (no key here). DNS: read for real (section 6). The new tables: applied to the live project and read back (section 5).
- **(c) Does the CRM already do it.** The CRM has no cross-account alert routing, no per-type quiet hours, no per-client Slack scoping and no agency-wide bell; that is the gap. It does not replace the CRM's own notifications.
- **(d) Simplest version that proves value.** Works with zero setup (defaults from the Slack channel, brief switch and hour, email address and quiet hours people already had). One matrix, one wording box per kind, one bell. Slack stays the smallest permission (one incoming webhook); several channels are several sign-ins.
- **(e) Risk.** Behaviour change on merge: every email and Slack post now goes through the switches, so **until `SEND_EMAIL_LIVE` and `SEND_SLACK_LIVE` are set to `true` in Vercel the existing daily Slack brief, the Monday email and the alert emails and Slack posts stop (they are logged as WOULD SEND)**. That is the lead's rule, but King must know before merging. Privacy: a message names client accounts, so notification text is in "What we store" and deleted on "Delete my data"; a client's staff see only their own account's bell items; a channel limited to one account never receives another's. Cost: none. Database: five new empty tables, applied.

## 3. What was built

**Pure core** (`src/lib/notifications/`, no database or network, runs under node's type stripping):
- `catalog.ts` the nine kinds (the eight asked for plus "Other alerts" for late replies, stale deals, churn risk and data quality, so no alert kind the app raises is dropped), their words, variables, defaults; tickets inactive.
- `copy.ts` the wording rules: plain text only (angle brackets refused), only the kind's own variables (an unknown name is refused with the list that works), one-pass rendering (data can never become a template), cleaned values.
- `timing.ts` quiet hours that wrap midnight, the chosen hour in the chosen zone, once-a-day and once-a-week batching, critical never held, scheduled briefs due from the hour until 3 hours later once per local date; daylight saving through the platform clock.
- `prefs.ts` defaults from the older settings, defensive reading of what is stored, checking of every change with a sentence per fault.
- `dispatcher.ts` `plan()` (one ledger row per place: bell, each email address, each eligible Slack channel; pending or held; a repeat creates nothing) and `drain()` (sends what is due, puts what is ready for one place into one message, retries 1/5/15/60 minutes up to 5 tries, stops at once on a refusal, skips what changed while it waited: switched off, fixed, unsubscribed, channel gone).
- `slack.ts` channel eligibility (a channel for one account only receives that account's messages; a message about all accounts never reaches it), the plain meaning of Slack's answers (including `missing_scope`), the post, the 2-minute wait. `test-send.ts` the allowlist guard and "send me a test". `bell.ts`, `compose.ts`, `brief-lines.ts`.

**Server:** `store.ts` (preferences, encrypted Slack channels, bell, ledger), `transports.ts` (email and Slack through `outward()`, the bell is our own database), `service.ts` (context, alerts, scheduled briefs, drain), `src/lib/api/notifications-api.ts` (endpoints), migration `20261102120000_f9_notifications.sql`.

## 6. DNS and deliverability (read-only lookups, 2 Nov 2026)

The sending address is not available in this worktree (`EMAIL_FROM` is not set locally), so I checked `kdsquares.com` and the Resend return-path subdomain that Resend always uses (`send.`).

| Record | Found |
| --- | --- |
| SPF, `kdsquares.com` | `v=spf1 include:dc-8e814c8572._spfm.kdsquares.com ~all`, which flattens to `v=spf1 include:zohomail.com ~all` (Zoho Mail only) |
| SPF, `send.kdsquares.com` (Resend's return path) | `v=spf1 include:dc-fd741b8612._spfm.send.kdsquares.com ~all`, which flattens to `include:amazonses.com`; MX `10 feedback-smtp.eu-west-1.amazonses.com` |
| DKIM, `resend._domainkey.kdsquares.com` | present (RSA key, `p=MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3...`), also `google._domainkey` (Google Workspace) |
| DMARC, `_dmarc.kdsquares.com` | `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:code@kdsquares.com; ruf=mailto:directors@kdsquares.com; sp=none; adkim=r; aspf=r` |
| MX, `kdsquares.com` | Zoho (`mx.zoho.com`, `mx2.zoho.com`, `mx3.zoho.com`) |

**Reasoning:** mail sent through Resend from an `@kdsquares.com` address passes DMARC because the DKIM signature (`resend._domainkey`) is signed for `kdsquares.com` and aligned (relaxed). SPF passes on the return path `send.kdsquares.com` (Amazon SES behind Resend) and aligns in relaxed mode. So the three records needed are all present and nothing is missing for delivery. **Not missing, but worth doing:** DMARC is in monitoring mode (`p=none`), so spoofing of the domain is not blocked and some mailbox providers weigh that. The exact record to move to once the reports sent to `code@kdsquares.com` look clean for a few weeks: `_dmarc.kdsquares.com TXT "v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; rua=mailto:code@kdsquares.com; adkim=r; aspf=r"` (then `p=reject`). Also: the apex SPF lists Zoho only, which is fine for Resend (it uses `send.`) but means a message sent from the apex by any other service (for example the CRM's own sending) would need to be added to that flattened record. **NOT VERIFIED LIVE:** inbox versus spam placement (needs a real send; morning check 4).

## FOR THE LEAD: METRICS/DECISIONS text

**METRICS:** no metric changed. (Alert thresholds are unchanged: speed breach at 3 or more leads past target, critical at 8; unmarked appointments at 5, critical at 20.)

**DECISIONS, 2 Nov 2026:**
- One dispatcher decides who is told what, when and where; every email and Slack post goes through the live switches (`outward()`), committed off. Reason: King's overnight rule, and it makes going live one controlled change.
- Slack stays the incoming-webhook method with one channel per sign-in (smallest permission); several channels are several destinations, each with its own scope (whole company, or one client account). Reason: the roadmap's own preference, and it makes "a client's Slack never receives another client's data" a rule of the data, not of the screen.
- A message to a place is recorded in a ledger with a unique key (installation, event, channel, place): dedupe, retries and quiet-hour holds are rows, so a restart or a double run cannot send twice.
- A critical alert is never held by quiet hours or by a once-a-day setting (a per-kind switch can turn that off). Reason: it was already the rule; a missed critical alert costs more than a night-time one.
- A scheduled brief is built at its chosen time and ignores quiet hours (the person chose the time).
- Live test emails: only to an allowlisted address, at most 5 in 24 hours, counted before the send, never when no mail service is configured; every other address is a dry run.
