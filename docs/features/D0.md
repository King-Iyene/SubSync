## 2. The five evaluation lines (principle 1)

- **(a) Decision and for whom.** Every user: where to look, and how to get to the lead behind a number in one click. King: whether the product looks and feels mature enough to show. Setters and realtors on a phone: what to do next (My day).
- **(b) Data truly available.** The components add no new data. They read what the existing endpoints already send (tested in the browser against the demo company on a clean dev server). Two small server additions, both read-only and scoped like the endpoints they extend: the filter options now list each account with its test and ignore flags (agency sessions only), and the Speed to lead trend now also sends the leads behind each week (same rows as the bar, tested). Not verified against a real company: the demo has no test account and no ignored account in the filter list.
- **(c) Does the CRM already do it.** The CRM has its own menus, lists and side panels; none of them work across client accounts or show the counted-lead definition. This stage is structure and look, not a feature the CRM does.
- **(d) Simplest version that proves value.** Plain HTML and CSS components and one chart approach (no library), tokens in one CSS file, pure logic in `src/lib/design/` so it is unit tested. No gauge, no pie (nothing in the app needs one yet), no new dependency.
- **(e) Risk.** Shell changes touch every page (menu, theme, filter, back, address). Mitigations: nothing removed (pages moved under More or into People), the old address forms still work, the theme default stays dark, every old test was kept (only the menu labels and the one strip test were updated for the new structure). The address now carries filter and ranges (`#speed?owner=Sam&r.speed-trend=30d.prev`); inputs are validated and a damaged link falls back to the default (tested). UNSURE items in section 10.

## 3. Design direction: one recommended, two alternatives (King could not be asked)

**Recommended and built: "Calm and exact".** Navy surfaces (#000C25 page, #031636 cards), one teal for what you can press (the exact brand teal #0A658B on light; a lighter tint of it, #4DB3DC, on navy because the exact teal is too dark to read there), gold #D4AD58 held back for the single recommended thing and plan marks (never a status), status colours only for status and always with words. Hierarchy comes from type size, weight, spacing and thin rules, not boxes: plain status text with a thin rule on its left, one-row notices with a rule, empty states between two rules, underline tabs, charts on a labelled scale from zero. Geist type, 12/14/16/26 px, 120 and 200 ms motion that stops for reduced-motion. Dark is the default (what people had before); light and "match my device" are one tap away in the menu.

**Alternative A, "Paper and ink".** Light first: warm off-white page, navy text, teal links, no dark mode by default. Pros: friendliest for small business owners and printing. Cons: loses the premium navy look King asked for, and every existing screen was tuned dark.

**Alternative B, "Signal".** Near-black with high-contrast teal and gold, dense tables, small type, monospace numbers. Pros: feels like an operations console for setters who live in it. Cons: harder on small screens and on older eyes, and gold would carry meaning on every screen so it stops being special.

King approved the recommended direction with the KF-1 correction. Change it at `/design`.

**Pure helpers (all unit tested).** `src/lib/design/filters.ts`: `visibleControls`, `describeHidden`, `scopeOf`, `nameList`, `filterToQuery`, `filterToLink`, `parsePickedAccounts`. `route.ts`: `parseHash`, `buildHash`. `range.ts`: `resolveRange`, `encodeRange`, `decodeRange`, `rangeFromHash`, `rangeIntoHash`, `rangesOf`, `changeBetween` (no percentage below a base of 10), `shareChange` (points, only with 10 or more on both sides), `inWindow`. `trend-bars.ts`: `weeksIn`, `weekBars`, `compareWeeks`. `ui-logic.ts`: `paginate`, `niceScale`, `waitLabel`, `errorId`, `neighbours`, `resolveTheme`. `color.ts`: `contrast`, `accentTokens`.

**How a page makes a chart clickable (the pattern, from Speed to lead).** (1) The server sends the rows and the count from the SAME selection (`judgedInWeek` in `src/lib/analytics/speed-intel.ts` feeds both the bar and its list). (2) The page builds `bars` with `count`. (3) `const drill = useDrill()`; `<BarChart onSelect={drill.select} selected={drill.selected} noun="leads" .../>`. (4) `<DrillPanel drill={drill} count={bar.count} rows={rowsOfThatBar} .../>`. (5) Write the unit test that the bar's count equals the rows (`src/lib/__tests__/trend-drill.test.ts` is the model). Scoping: the rows come from the same endpoint and the same `allowedIds` as the number, so a client's staff only ever receive their own account's rows.

**How a page uses a card range.** `const range = useCardRange("my-card")`; draw `<CardRange state={range} label="My card" />`; ask for or filter exactly `range.resolved.current` (and `.compare`); show `changeBetween(...)` or `shareChange(...)` text, never your own percentage.
