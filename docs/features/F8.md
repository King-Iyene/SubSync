## 2. The five evaluation lines (principle 1)

- **(a) Decision and for whom.** A first-time agency owner deciding "can I trust this with my clients' data, and what do I do first?" (Trust page, Settings, Getting started). A setter or a client's staff who only wants their list (a three-step guide, no company settings). King deciding what to build next (votes and suggestions with an admin view) and where the product is failing (feedback and problem reports with error IDs).
- **(b) Data truly available.** Everything here is SubSync's own database and code. The granted permissions come from `installations.scope` (the space-separated list the platform returned at install; the two real installs granted 21 and 19 of 21, from `docs/CAPABILITIES.md`). Not verified live: that `contacts.write` is the permission the platform requires for `POST /contacts/{id}/notes` (the code path exists and stores a refusal as "needs permission"; docs/SCOPE_PLAN.md and docs/INTEGRATIONS_SPEC.md section 14 name it). The Settings panel for the real install can only show what the stored scope string says. The migration was applied to the live database (two empty tables); no database or browser test was run (parallel mode).
- **(c) Does the CRM already do it.** No. The CRM has its own settings; none of it is about SubSync. A trust page, roadmap and feedback box are the product's own.
- **(d) Simplest version that proves value.** One list per concern in `src/lib` (sections, guide steps, scope groups, Trust claims, wording), each unit tested, each read by the screen; a feedback table and a suggestions table; the existing audit log for everything outward and for the settings history; the existing `ignored` switch for test accounts (no new mechanism).
- **(e) Risk.** Trust: a Trust page that is wrong is worse than none, so every claim has a proof and the unprovable ones are shown as promises (the incident wording and the compliance line need King's approval). Privacy: free text typed by customers is stored (feedback), listed in "What we store", removed on uninstall, never in logs or audit lines (tested). Change risk: Settings and Getting started are rewritten; every old control keeps its id and label (tested), and the three browser specs and one integration script that counted the old page were updated. Nothing outward: no email, Slack or CRM write was built.

## 3. What was built

**Settings (`src/app/app/views/settings.tsx`, `settings-parts.tsx`, `src/lib/settings-sections.ts`).** Eight sections with a left menu (a row of tabs on a phone). One form and one Save button: a change in one section is kept while another is open and the page says "You have changes that are not saved yet"; Save is disabled until something changed; the success line says "recalculated" only when a number-changing setting was saved (`NUMBER_SETTINGS`, tested against the server's own list). The open section is remembered in this browser; Getting started opens the right one. Team and access: client access switches, **Test and practice accounts** (new), and **Who can open SubSync**. Notifications: Slack, daily brief, weekly email, quiet hours, send-at. Integrations: the E0 slot, the Connections card, and **What SubSync can read** (the permission groups with approved or "Needs permission: reconnect"). Reply targets and thresholds: the old two cards, now saying "You choose these numbers. SubSync does not read them from your CRM". Brand: name, colour, and a preview. Data and privacy: meeting notes privacy, What we store (with a link to the Trust page), Delete my data. Sync status: the F5 slot, then the S0.4 panel. Advanced: Links, the checklist, the AI connector keys, and **History of changes**.

**Audit of settings changes.** `settings.changed` (names only: a Slack address is never in a line), `accounts.flags_changed` (client access, owner label, leave-out), `apikey.created` and `apikey.revoked` are written to `audit_log` with who and when (`src/lib/feedback-queries.ts`, called from the router). Advanced shows them in plain words (`describeChange`). Delete my data was already audited (`data.purged`, `data.purge_refused`).

**Test accounts (N1).** A list of every client account with a switch "Leave out of every number". It is the existing account switch (`PUT accounts/{id}/flags {ignored}`): the queries already exclude an ignored account everywhere (numbers, alerts, lists, reports). SubSync only suggests accounts whose name says test, demo, sample or sandbox, with a one-click "Leave them out"; the owner decides. Agency sessions only (the route policy refuses it for a business or a client). Getting started has a step for it that is done when nothing looks like a test account or one is left out.

**One wording for writing to the CRM (N77, `src/lib/meeting-wording.ts`).** The Meeting notes page used to say "SubSync does not write into your CRM" while Settings and Connections said it does. One module now holds the true sentence (a matched contact gets one note, with the add-notes permission, if the setting is on), and Settings, Connections, the Meeting notes page, the help answer, the install confirmation page and Getting started read it. With the permission not granted, the Settings checkbox is disabled and says "Needs permission: reconnect" (the server now sends `noteWriteGranted` from `installations.scope`). A test fails if any source file says SubSync does not write to the CRM.

**Scope experience (N64, `src/lib/scope-groups.ts`, `docs/SCOPES.md`).** The 21 permissions in 7 groups (Your client accounts, Contacts, Deals, Conversations and calls, Appointments, Team, Tags fields and tasks), each with a plain reason, required or optional, read or change, plus Email health (needs no permission) and a "not requested, and why" list. `docs/SCOPES.md` is generated from it (`scripts/gen-scopes-doc.ts`) and a test fails when it is stale. `permissionPrompt(feature, granted)` gives the one prompt. The Appointments and Speed to lead screens used "Needs approval" and "needs permission"; both now say "Needs permission: reconnect" (two one-line edits).

**Getting started (`src/lib/getting-started.ts`, `views/help.tsx`, `views/step-art.tsx`).** A guide per role (agency owner or administrator 13 steps with the meeting step while that page is on; agency staff 6, written but unreachable while staff are refused at sign-in; business administrator 11; a client's staff 5). Each step has a drawing (small inline SVG, a gentle pulse that stops for reduced motion), a short body, "Do it now" (opens the page, and for Settings the exact section), and ticks only from facts: pages opened, settings saved (read from the audit lines), Slack and email set, an audit shared, accounts left out, feedback sent. A client's staff do not use the company's page counter (it is the agency's); their steps tick from the pages opened in their own browser. Steps a client must not see (Slack, email, targets, shared audits, client access) do not exist in their guide.

**Feedback (`src/app/app/feedback-box.tsx`, `src/lib/feedback.ts`, `feedback-queries.ts`, `api/f8-routes.ts`).** "Give feedback" and "Report a problem" at the bottom of every page (two text buttons, a small form in place). Feedback has a severity; a problem report carries the error ID the screen showed (the error state remembers it and has a "Report this problem" button that opens the form with the ID filled). Stored in `feedback_items` for the signed-in person's own company; a reference "R-XXXXXX" comes back. Twenty a day per person. Nothing is sent: each submission writes a `notify.king.would_send` audit line with ids only (tested: the text typed is in no audit line; the source has no email, Slack or fetch import).

**Roadmap (`src/lib/roadmap.ts`, `views/roadmap.tsx`).** Items grouped by status word with what each means (Planned, In progress, Considering, Shipped); parked work (benchmarks, the AI brief) can only be "Considering"; every item names its stage in `docs/ROADMAP_V2.md` (tested); nothing is "In progress" because nothing is being built that the page can honestly claim. One vote per person per item (the database key), taken back with a second press, none on a shipped item. "Suggest something" (title, details, how much it matters) stored in `roadmap_suggestions`; a person sees only their own. The heading now says "Votes help us decide what to build next." King's admin view: `GET /api/admin/feedback` (the server secret only) and `scripts/feedback-report.ts` (votes by item and company, suggestions, feedback and problems; companies as short ids). Dry-run notices: a suggestion or problem writes an instant `WOULD SEND` line, a vote a digest line, and `scripts/feedback-report.ts --shipped <key>` writes one "it is live" line per company that voted.

**Trust page (`/trust`, `src/lib/trust.ts`, `src/lib/company.ts`).** Ten sections: what SubSync reads (the permissions, grouped, with reasons), what it stores (generated), for how long, how to delete it, which outside services handle data (Vercel, Supabase, Resend, your CRM, and Slack and Fathom only if connected), security practices, what happens if something goes wrong, is SubSync working (honest: there is no public status page yet; Settings, Sync status is the answer today), connecting Fathom (the "not yet reviewed by Fathom" notice explained, plus the consent line), and who we are. Every sentence is derived from tested code, a proven claim, or a promise shown under "What we promise". `src/lib/__tests__/trust-truth.test.ts` runs 11 checks, including a proof per claim, a check that the proofs can fail, no mention of anything switched off, the permission list equal to the install link, every outside host the code talks to accounted for, and the company name and support address identical on every public page.

**Slots.** `settings-slot-watching.tsx` (F5: replace the body with `<WatchingPanel />`) sits above the sync panel; `settings-slot-connections.tsx` (E0: My connections and Team connections) sits above the connection cards. Both render nothing now.

**Files that are shared hot spots, touched minimally:** `dashboard.tsx` (the feedback box and the page-seen memory: 4 lines), `router.ts` (new dispatch lines, the start, roadmap and settings views, audit calls), `access.ts` (feedback and problem routes allowed for business and client), `states.tsx` (the error ID memory and the Report button), `legal-page.tsx` (a Trust link), `appointments.tsx` and `speed.tsx` (one line each: the permission words).

## 8. King's 5 checks (plain words, in order)

1. **Settings.** Open Settings. On the left are eight sections. Open Reply targets and thresholds, change the reply target, then open Brand and come back: your change is still there and the page says it is not saved. Press Save. Open Advanced: History of changes lists "Changed settings: Reply target (hours)" with your name and the time, and no value. Put the target back.
2. **Test accounts.** Settings, Team and access, Test and practice accounts. If an account's name says test, a line offers "Leave it out". Switch one on: Today and Overview stop counting it. Switch it back. Nothing is deleted.
3. **Meeting notes wording.** Settings, Data and privacy (needs a connected meeting tool to show) and Connections: both say the same sentence about adding a note to a matched contact. If your install did not grant the add-notes permission, the checkbox is greyed and says "Needs permission: reconnect".
4. **Getting started.** Open it as yourself: you get the owner's steps, the next step is marked, and "Do it now" on "Set your reply targets" lands on that Settings section. Open SubSync as a client's staff member: five steps, nothing about Slack, targets or other clients. Then at the bottom of any page press Give feedback, send a line, and note the reference; press Report a problem.
5. **Trust and roadmap.** Open `/trust` (log out first if you like). Read "What we promise": it should be only things software cannot check. Check the company line is right. On What's new, vote on one item and take it back, suggest something, and read the meaning of "Considering". Then run `node --env-file=.env.local --import tsx scripts/feedback-report.ts` to see what you sent.
