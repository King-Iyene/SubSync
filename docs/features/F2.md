# F2: Speed to lead

Status: built and verified (31 Oct 2026). Evidence at the bottom.

## Goal

The best screen in the product is correct on messy real data.

## What the code did at the start of the stage (found by reading it)

1. A lead was any contact created in the last 14 days that has a conversation. Spam, test contacts, people who asked not to be contacted, repeats of the same person and bulk imports that later got a message were all counted.
2. Only messages counted as a first reply; a phone call placed by a person did not.
3. No handling of nights and weekends: a lead that arrived on Saturday afternoon showed as "waiting" for two days and could raise a breach alert at 2am.
4. Facebook and Instagram lead-form leads shared a bucket with direct messages (60 minute target), although they asked for contact like a form.
5. No sentence in plain words at the top, and no explanation of how many contacts were left out.
6. The demo had no messy leads and no real estate example.

## Decisions

- **Lead rules in one place** (`src/lib/analytics/lead-rules.ts`): bulk import, spam, test, do not contact, duplicate, first match wins; stored as `lead_touches.excluded` after every speed sync, so every screen, the breach alert, the team figures and the audit read the same set. The screen says how many contacts were left out and why.
- **Calls count** as a first touch when a person placed them (answered or not); inbound calls and calls with no person do not.
- **Business hours** (off by default, in Settings): a lead that arrives while the team is closed starts its clock at the next opening in that account's own time zone, Monday to Friday, default 9 to 17. Stored as `lead_touches.clock_start`. Known limit: a lead that arrives in hours and is still unanswered at closing keeps counting overnight (shown as waiting, never hidden). A full business-minutes clock was not built: it would need the same logic in SQL and in code.
- **Meta lead forms are their own source** (Facebook and Instagram lead forms, target 10 minutes). Messages to the page stay "social" (60). The classifier reads the contact's source text; how the CRM records Meta attribution could not be checked on live data because none of the connected accounts has a Meta lead (CHECK stays open).
- **example.com is not a test signal:** it is the placeholder address in sample data, including the demo.
- **A plain sentence first:** "15 leads are waiting for a reply, and 42 were never answered."
- **Real estate sample:** `scripts/make-sample-audit.ts` publishes a fictional four-office real estate audit at `/audit/sample-real-estate-team-audit` (Facebook and Instagram lead forms waiting, stuck deals, unmarked showings, unrebooked no-shows). Every number is invented and the page says so. No lead capture or lead management was built.

## Edge cases (tests)

- Each exclusion reason, the order when several apply, "Contest Winners" and "Attestation Law" are not test names, 19 contacts in a minute are people and 20 are an import, "Importance Realty" is not an import.
- A repeated email or phone: only the earlier contact counts.
- A contact tagged spam after the lead was recorded drops out on the next run; a second run changes nothing.
- Weekend, Friday evening, before opening, at closing, other time zones, a daylight-saving change over the weekend (New York, 1 Nov 2026), an unknown time zone.
- Outbound call by a person, outbound call by a workflow (no person), inbound call, workflow text.
- Facebook Lead Form (meta, not web form), Instagram DM (social), a form whose name contains "Lead Form" (web form).
- Under 5 judged leads: "Not enough data" (existing rule); never answered after 3 days (existing rule); lead with no owner (existing rule).

## Acceptance criteria

1. All edge-case tests green (unit, real database, browser).
2. The screen's numbers match raw database rows (`scripts/check-speed.ts`), including the exclusions and the clock start.
3. Screenshots at three widths in `docs/review/`.
4. `npm run verify` and `npm run e2e` green.

### Bug found by the real-database test

The first version of the rules update used the wrong id type and failed silently (the error was caught and logged, as intended for a background job), so no lead was ever excluded. The browser tests caught it on the demo; the real-database test now pins it.

### Self-review (S0 blocks release, S1 fix before pilot, S2 fix soon, S3 note)

- **S1: the Meta lead-form source is unconfirmed on live data.** The classifier looks for Facebook, Instagram or Meta in the contact's source text. If the CRM records those leads under a form name only, they land in "web forms". Needs one real Meta lead to confirm.
- **S1: calls were not seen live.** The platform's call message shape (type TYPE_CALL, a user, a direction) comes from its documentation and the earlier probe; no connected account has an outbound call in the synced window, so call touches are proven by unit tests only.
- **S2: business hours is an approximation** (start-of-clock shift, not a business-minutes clock), and uses Monday to Friday only. Documented in Settings and METRICS.md.
- **S2: two installations of the same account double count** in totals. The real KD Squares LTD account shows each lead twice because two installations hold it; each installation is its own tenant and sees its own, so no customer sees double, but the owner's database view does.
- **S2:** the 14-day window and the first-100-messages limit are unchanged (see METRICS.md).
- **S3:** demo figures are synthetic: Meta lead forms at 0% in target is the seed's random reply times, not a claim about real teams.
- No S0 findings.
