## 2. The five evaluation lines (principle 1)

- **(a) Decision and for whom.** Every agency and business deciding whether to trust a number on screen: is it fresh or not. King deciding whether the product is "monitoring" (minutes) or a daily report. A setter chasing a waiting lead needs minutes-old data.
- **(b) Data truly available.** Yes, tested: the live `sync_jobs` table (97 and 90 finished cycles in 55 and 32 hours) and `locations` timestamps are the source; Vercel's own cron documentation was read for the plan limits. Webhook events: the live `webhook_events` table shows which event types really arrive (see section 3). Not verifiable from here: which events the marketplace app is subscribed to (the portal is King's).
- **(c) Does the CRM already do it.** No: the CRM does not tell a user how fresh a third-party app's copy is.
- **(d) Simplest version that proves value.** One pure function for freshness used by every screen and the daily brief; the existing queue and the existing cron kept (they work on Pro); the cadence bug fixed; one panel instead of three cards.
- **(e) Risk.** Cost: cron invocations are ordinary function invocations (every 5 minutes is 288 a day). Behaviour change on merge: the webhook route will now act on the real `INSTALL` and `UNINSTALL` event names (see section 3). Privacy: the alarm and the diagnostics hold ids and times only.

## 3. Diagnosis from the real data (instruction 1)

Source: the live `sync_jobs` table, read with `scripts/sync-timeline.ts` (read only; the full output of the run of 1 Nov 2026 12:10 UTC is the basis of the tables below; rerun it any time).

**Hosting plan.** Vercel's cron documentation (read 1 Nov 2026): Hobby, minimum interval once per day, "cron expressions that would run more frequently will fail during deployment"; Pro, once per minute, 100 cron jobs a project, included in the plan, each run billed as an ordinary function invocation. `vercel.json` has asked for `*/5 * * * *` since the first commit (29 Oct).

**What the job table shows, per install** ("cycle" = one refresh round, recorded by its `metrics` job; "gap" = time between two cycles being queued):

| Install | Cycles in the last 48 h | Before 06:25 UTC on 31 Oct | From 06:25 UTC on 31 Oct to now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agency install `a1c7eb2e` (3 accounts) | 95 | 5 gaps over 30 minutes: 82, 123, 181, 489 and 180 minutes | 86 of 89 gaps are 16 to 20 minutes, the other 3 are shorter or a little longer (up to 23 minutes, after a manual Sync now); median 20, none over 30 |
| Business install `a82b0b8b` (1 account) | 92 | one gap of 200 minutes (its first cycle to the next) | 89 of 90 gaps are 16 to 20 minutes (one 8 minute gap after a manual press); median 20, none over 30 |

Last 3 hours before the fix (agency install): 9 cycles, every one exactly 20 minutes after the one before (`09:25:48, 09:45:48, ... 12:05:48`), each finished within 40 seconds. No failed job exists in the table (none, in the whole life of the table). Nothing was waiting or running at the time of the read.

**Findings.**
1. **Why it was irregular before 06:25 UTC on 31 Oct:** the 5 minute cron could not run (a Hobby plan allows one run a day and refuses a more frequent schedule at deploy time), so data moved only when somebody pressed Sync now or a webhook queued a read: the bursts 82 to 489 minutes apart. This matches King's N33 and N62 observations (the "Last synced" clock growing, about hourly). INFERENCE: that the plan changed to Pro at that moment (the first steady cycle is 06:25:48 UTC on 31 Oct); the Vercel dashboard is King's and was not read.
2. **Not a stuck queue, not a manual-button-only job:** from 06:25 UTC the scheduler queued a cycle on its own every time, jobs started within 0.5 minutes and finished within one minute, zero failures. Jobs are claimed by the cron worker, not only by the button (the button just runs a short slice sooner).
3. **A real bug: the steady cadence was 20 minutes, not 15.** A cycle was due "15 minutes after the last one finished"; the scheduler wakes on a 5 minute grid and a cycle finishes some seconds after its wake-up, so the tick 15 minutes later fell a few seconds short and the next one (5 minutes later) was used. Reproduced in `src/lib/__tests__/schedule.test.ts` (the old rule gives 20 minutes in a fake-clock run). Fixed by measuring from when the last cycle was queued with a one minute allowance.
4. **The webhook route ignored real install and uninstall events.** The live `webhook_events` table (kept 3 days) holds `INSTALL` x10, `UNINSTALL` x7, `UPDATE` x3, `ContactUpdate` x1, `ContactCreate` x1; the code only knew `AppInstall` and `AppUninstall`. Fixed (`canonicalType`).
5. **Appointment webhooks queued the wrong read** (`AppointmentCreate` refreshed account details instead of appointments, and update and delete events were not handled). Fixed; message events also queue the reply-time read.

**What the existing history proves, and what it cannot.** It proves the steady state (every 20 minutes for 30 hours on two installs, zero failures, jobs finished within a minute), the early bursts, and that the freshness definition (oldest account's last conversations read) tracks real rows: at 12:10 UTC the screen sentence for the agency install worked out as "Updated 4 min ago" with the three accounts last refreshed 12:06:10, 12:06:11 and 12:06:12. It cannot prove the new 15 minute cadence (the fix is not deployed: it takes effect on merge), real webhook-driven refreshes of appointments, deals or messages (no such event arrived in the window), a real uninstall event now removing data, or the alarm on a real outage. Only a few hours after the merge can prove the 15 minute cadence: `node --env-file=.env.local --import tsx scripts/sync-timeline.ts --hours 6` must show a median gap of 15 and no gap over 30. The timeline script is in `scripts/sync-timeline.ts`.

## 4. What was built

- **Scheduler fix** (`src/lib/sync/schedule.ts`, `scheduleFastRefresh` in `src/lib/sync/engine.ts`): one place for the cadence constants; due 14 minutes after the last cycle was queued, never while jobs are queued or running; scheduler wake every 5 minutes kept. Choice and reasoning in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
- **Webhooks** (`src/lib/ghl/webhook.ts`, `src/app/api/webhooks/ghl/route.ts`, `enqueueAreaSync`): INSTALL and UNINSTALL understood; appointment events read appointments; message and conversation events read conversations and reply times; the dedupe on the webhook id is unchanged.
- **One freshness source** (`src/lib/sync/freshness.ts`, `freshness-queries.ts`): the oldest account's last successful refresh, exact wording, stale and failed wording, plain failure reasons, the running note only for a first or 10-minute-plus sync. Served by `GET freshness` (every kind of person) and inside Today, Overview, the Slack brief and the Getting started step.
- **One line on every page** (`FreshnessStrip` in `src/app/app/views/sync-status.tsx`, drawn once in `dashboard.tsx`), with "Each account" underneath. Removed: the badge and banner on Today, the Overview banner and Data sync card, the Settings Connection health and Data sync cards, the old vague helper and its test.
- **Sync status panel** (`SyncStatusPanel`, `GET sync-status`, agency and business only): the sentence, next refresh, checks in the last 24 hours with a plain explanation of what a check is, "what went wrong" with plain reasons, a table per account with Refresh buttons, "what runs, and when", and Sync now.
- **Alarm and diagnostics** (`alarm.ts`, `watchdog.ts`, `api/cron/watchdog`, `api/admin/diagnostics`): audit lines `sync.alarm` and `sync.recovered`, an hourly reminder while it stands, notification a dry run; diagnostics with the server secret only.
- **Send at** (`src/lib/analytics/digest-intel.ts`, `weekly-email.ts`, `schedule.ts`): hourly checks, one send per local date, in the chosen hour or the next 3, never past the local day; the Settings note says so.
- **Resolved errors clear** (`clearResolvedError`): an account's `sync_error` is cleared once its latest finished jobs no longer fail.
- **Tests and tools:** listed in the evidence table. `scripts/sync-timeline.ts` prints the timeline; `scripts/with-dev-server.mjs` gives the local test server a made-up cron secret and runs the new `check-sync` check.

## 6. King's 5 checks (in plain words)

After the merge, give it two or three hours first (so the new 15 minute rhythm has run), then:

1. **The line above every page.** Open SubSync from your CRM menu, on any page. Above the title there should be exactly one grey line, "Updated N min ago" (N under 20 most of the time). Click "Each account": it lists your accounts with their own times. The old "Data is under an hour old", "Last synced" and "A sync is still running" must be nowhere. Click through five different pages: the line must be identical.
2. **Compare three timestamps with the CRM.** Pick one account. In your CRM, send yourself a test reply or note a time in a conversation (or just look at the newest message in the inbox and its time). Press Refresh for that account in Settings, Sync status. The account's "Last refreshed" must be within a minute of now, and the newest conversation in SubSync (Inbox & SLA) must be the same one as in the CRM inbox. Write down: the time on the line, the time in the Sync status table, and the time of the newest message in the CRM. They should agree within the 15 minutes.
3. **The rhythm.** Run `node --env-file=.env.local --import tsx scripts/sync-timeline.ts --hours 3` on your computer. "Gap between cycles: median 15 min" and "Gaps over 30 minutes: 0".
4. **Settings, Sync status.** One panel (no Connection health, no Data sync). It says the time, "Next refresh in about N minutes", what a check is, and "What runs, and when". Press Sync now and watch it say "Sync started"; the line above stays truthful.
5. **Send at.** In Settings, set "Send at" to the next full hour in your own time zone, with the Slack channel connected and "Send the daily brief to Slack" on. The brief should arrive in that hour (or within three hours if the hourly check is late), once, and not again that day. (This is the only check that sends a real message to your Slack; do it on a channel you own.)
