## 2. The five evaluation lines (principle 1)

(a) **Decision and for whom.** The agency owner and a business owner decide where to spend effort and whether to believe a number. Every one of these fixes protects that decision: a dollar figure, a waiting count, a health label or a forecast that disagrees with another screen, or is built on junk, makes the product untrustworthy to a buyer in the first five minutes. Staff decide from the Inbox, Speed to lead and Leads lists.
(b) **Is the data available in the live API with our scopes?** Tested through what the production sync stored on 1 Nov 2026 (two real installs, 21 and 19 permissions granted, all access flags `ok`) rather than assumed: deals carry value, owner and stage dates; contacts carry no last-activity field (0 of 52,926), so "quiet" is built from conversations, appointments and deals instead; conversations carry unread counts and channel but a direction on only 698 of 1,265; deals have no last-action date and no stage history (SubSync records its own). Live probes from this machine were impossible (the real tokens cannot be opened here), so the live column is NOT VERIFIED LIVE and one call after the merge settles it (`docs/CAPABILITIES.md`).
(c) **Does the CRM already do it, or a competitor?** The CRM's own reports are per account and do not classify stages or forecast with a stated range; the forecast and the stage rules are the part SubSync adds across accounts. A forecast is commodity in outline, so it is shown only with its method and range, as a trust feature rather than a headline feature.
(d) **Simplest version that proves value.** Rules and honesty, not new screens: one definition of a counted deal in one SQL fragment; one unread number; a label that cannot contradict the waiting list; a forecast that says how sure it is; the owner's rules as one table on the Pipeline page.
(e) **Risk.** Data: the new `stage_rules` table holds pipeline and stage names and the owner's win probabilities (disclosed in "What we store", checked by the schema test); no customer data. Scope: none added, none needed. Cost: a few more joins in the account totals query (about 3.4 seconds for the largest real install, three round trips of 0.4 seconds each from this machine). Correctness risk: changing what "counted" means changes totals on every screen; mitigated by one fragment, golden-deal tests, an HTTP test of every page under five filters, and definition versions so history is not mixed. Trust risk: auto-classifying stages by name could wrongly close out a real stage; mitigated by the owner's override and the screen stating which stages were decided by name.
