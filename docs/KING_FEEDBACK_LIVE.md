# King's live feedback during the overnight run (every stage agent reads this)

Each item has an ID (KF-n), who must act, and the stage that owns it. King sent these while the run was going; they override older wording where they conflict.

## KF-1. Pills and banners look "AI" (stage D0, then everyone)
The status badges (rounded tinted pill with a small coloured dot, for example "never answered", "waiting 139 h") and the alert and error banners look generic. No dot, no tinted pill, no big tinted rounded box: use plain status-coloured text or a thin rule/marker, plain one-line banners with the problem in words and an error ID. Hierarchy through type, weight, spacing and thin dividers. King approved the rest of the design (so D0's style is approved with this one change). Owner: D0 (components), all later stages must use the shared badge and banner.

## KF-2. The client-account filter must show WHICH accounts are in it (stage D0, F5)
Today the filter bar says "Client accounts 3 of 13" and "Showing 3 of 13 client accounts. Show all". It must list the names of the accounts currently included (for example as removable names or a short list that expands), and say which are left out and why (test account, set to ignore, no access). Owner: D0 (shared filter bar), F5 for Today.

## KF-3. Dialing must use the CRM's own dialer, not the phone's native dialer (stage F10, also F2 and F3 for "call" buttons, N20, N53, N55)
Wherever SubSync has a call action (Speed to lead, Appointments, Call lists, the lead drawer, People), the button must open the CRM's own dialer for that contact through the white-label link helper (`crmUrl` / `deepLinks` in `src/lib/links.ts`), not the device's native phone app (`tel:`). Verify what link opens the dialer for a contact; if the CRM has no link that opens its dialer, say so plainly in the UI and the log ("Not available from your CRM yet") and fall back to opening the contact in the CRM, not to `tel:`. A user-chosen setting "Dialer": "My CRM's dialer" (default) or "My own phone app / own dialer (phone link)" is allowed, as King said "the GHL dialer, or their own dialer" (a third-party dialer such as Kixie later, after checking its API). DND and quiet-hours warning before any dial. No telephony built by us.

## KF-4. Granular filters and comparisons inside each section, not only in the page header (stage D0 component; F2, F3, F6, F4, F5, F10, C1 use it)
Every card or chart that shows a number over time needs its own small range control (Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, custom) and a comparison choice (previous period, same period last month/year) where it makes sense, in addition to the page-level filter bar. The window must always be stated on the card ("Last 30 days, 1 Oct to 30 Oct, compared with 1 Sep to 30 Sep"). The card-level control overrides the page range for that card only, is carried in the link, and respects the minimum-sample rules (no percentage change on a tiny base). Owner: D0 builds `CardRange` (or equivalent) and the comparison helper; each page stage uses it on every card that needs it.

## KF-5. Think like the customer and make things easier (every stage)
King's instruction: do not stop at the listed items. Put yourself in the customer's seat (the personas in `docs/BUYER_AND_UX_REVIEW.md`: P1 skeptical agency owner, P2 ops person, P3 setter, P4 local business owner, P5 white-label owner) and, for the screens your stage touches, add the improvements that make their life easier. This is King's approval for small, safe, in-scope improvements; examples: every empty state says what to do next with one button; a plain one-line "what this means / what to do" on each number or chart; sensible defaults so nothing needs setup before it is useful; the most important action is the biggest thing on the screen and one click away; "next best thing to do" suggestions; remembering the last filter or range; keyboard-friendly lists; copy-to-clipboard and print/share where a person would otherwise retype; short in-place guides (a two-line "how to use this" that can be dismissed) and links to the Getting started step; friendlier error messages with a retry button; fewer clicks to get from an alert to the lead.
Guardrails: nothing outward-facing (no sending, no new connection to an outside service, no CRM writes, no new GHL scope, no marketplace change, no billing); no new tables or big features without the stage's scope; keep each improvement small and tested. Anything bigger or riskier is written as a PROPOSAL in `docs/PROPOSALS.md` (what, which persona, why it matters, effort, risk) for King to approve, never built. In the stage's feature file add a table "Customer-eyes improvements" (what was changed, which persona it helps, where) and list the proposals.

## KF-6. Charts must be clickable: click a bar, slice or point to see the leads, click a lead to see its details (D0 component; every stage wires it)
King's instruction: in the bar charts, pie/donut charts and other charts, clicking a bar, slice or point opens the list of leads (or appointments, deals, conversations) behind it, and clicking a row opens the lead drawer with its details. This is the type of idea King expects from the team. Rules: the chart component takes an `onSelect` (or a `drill` definition: the filter that produces the list) and the shared list drawer shows exactly the rows that make up that number, with the count equal to the number on the chart (an automated test per chart: the count on the chart equals the rows in the drilled list, using the same query/filter, never a second calculation); the drilled list keeps the page's filters and the card's range; the lead drawer has previous/next arrows through the drilled list; Back returns to the chart with the selection kept; keyboard accessible (focus, Enter) and each clickable mark has an accessible label ("Waiting 4 to 24 hours: 13 conversations, open the list"); a hover/tap tooltip shows the value and says it can be clicked. Clients (account users) only drill into their own account's rows (server-side scoping, tested). Owner: D0 builds the clickable chart components, the drill-down list and the lead drawer wiring; F2, F3, F6, F4, F5, F10, C1 and the others use them for every chart they ship.

## KF-7. Clients module: fees and renewals are invisible and unexplained (stage C1; the demo seed in D0/C1)
King looked at the demo Clients page: Fee, Renewal and "Value created" say "not set" / "needs a fee", the tiles say "n/a", and nothing explains how it works or where to start. Facts: fees, renewal dates, industry and notes are typed by the user (Details panel per client); SubSync does not read them from the CRM; the demo seed sets none. Required in C1: (1) demo clients get realistic sample fees, renewal dates, industries and a few logged conversations so the screen looks real; (2) first-use guidance instead of bare n/a: "Add your first client fee" with one button, a two-line explanation of every column and tile (what it is, where the data comes from, what to do), and the "Value created" formula in plain words; (3) fee, renewal date and industry editable inline in the row (no hunting for a panel), with a short "how this works" guide that can be dismissed and a Getting started step; (4) honest labels: "You enter this; SubSync does not read it from your CRM". Also applies to any other screen where a number depends on something only the user can enter (for example reply targets, stage win chances, test-account marks): say so on the screen and give the one-click way to enter it.
