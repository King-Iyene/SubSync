# Today buyer test (5 Nov 2026)

Honest limit: these are AI personas reading the redesigned page and the demo data. They find confusion and missing words. They cannot prove anyone will pay. Real proof is paid audits and five agency owners, as in the master brief.

Scores 1 to 5: do (tells me what to do), trust (in the numbers), speed (to understand), pay ($9 per client account). First number is the first read, second is after the fixes below.

## 1. Agency owner, 10 client accounts
- First 10 seconds: "78 people are waiting for a reply across 13 clients" says what is wrong. The button "Open Bright Smile Dental" and the first row "Review 2 quiet deals" say what to do next. Money is in the row ($1,004,011).
- Rows read verb first, with the client and the age in a few words, and one tap to open in the CRM.
- Would pay for: the one list across all clients, and the proof under Break it down for client reviews.
- Confusing: the sentence is about replies but the top row is about money (the list is sorted by amount, and the line under "Do this first" says so). Reply rows have no dollar figure.
- Would close the page: if the numbers did not match their CRM. Every number opens its list.
- Scores: do 4 / 4, trust 3 / 4, speed 4 / 4, pay 3 / 3.

## 2. Setter in one client account
- First 10 seconds: "7 people are waiting for a reply", button "Reply to Evelyn Santos". Rows: "Reply to Harper Nguyen", "New lead from Instagram Lead Ad", "Never answered". One tap to Reply.
- Calls for today are verbs too: "Call Ryan Hayes", "Confirm with Evelyn Bell".
- Would pay for: nothing, the setter does not buy. They would keep the page open all day.
- Confusing: "Answered in time 0%" with no reason (fixed: the info icon says what it counts).
- Would close the page: a page that scrolls past the first five rows. Five rows then "Show all 7".
- Scores: do 5 / 5, trust 4 / 4, speed 5 / 5, pay 2 / 2 (not their budget; not a UI problem).

## 3. Small business owner
- First 10 seconds: same sentence. "Reply to Evelyn Santos" is clear. "Quiet deals $61,180" needed a word (fixed: info icon, "money that could be lost, not a forecast").
- Confusing: "Open" did not say where it goes (fixed: hover text "Opens in your CRM"). "Needs an outcome" is clearer than "appointments to mark".
- Would pay for: being told, once a day, who has not been answered. $9 is fine if it replaces checking by hand.
- Would close the page: more than a screen of figures. This week is one chart and three figures, with the rest under Break it down.
- Scores: do 4 / 5, trust 3 / 4, speed 4 / 4, pay 3 / 4.

## What was fixed because of this test
1. Rows were nouns (a client name, "Waiting for a reply"). They are verbs now: "Reply to 13 people", "Rebook 3 no-shows", "Mark 2 appointments", "Review 2 quiet deals", "Call Ryan Hayes".
2. Explanations moved off the page into info icons and hover text (reply time, needs a reply, quiet deals, show rate, answered in time, needs an outcome). The headline line is one short line.
3. The CRM button says where it opens.

## Still open
- Reply rows carry no money, so they sort below deal rows. A real money figure for replies needs lead value from the CRM. Not built.
- Pay scores of 3 or lower for the setter and the agency owner are not UI faults. Test with real people.
