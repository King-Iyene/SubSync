# Appointments: the money test, and a review of the old screen (5 Nov 2026)

King's words: "customer first, money first (is this sellable, does it resolve a pain point, will customers buy); sexy and lovable, not overwhelming; works for agencies AND client-account users and on a phone; add the features that are missing; one-click dialing and emailing from every lead through the CRM's own dialer; the lead panel shows tags with the day each was added, and a timeline."

## Part 1. The money-first test (in plain words)

**1. The pain it removes.** Two things hide revenue in a calendar, and a third wastes it.
- **No-shows nobody follows up.** A person who booked a consultation and did not come is the warmest lead there is: they already said yes once. Most teams call them the same day or never. A no-show that is not called in the first days is usually lost.
- **Appointments nobody marks.** After the time passes, staff forget to set showed or no-show in the CRM. The calendar still says "confirmed". Every report built on it lies: the show rate looks better than it is, the no-shows are invisible, and nobody calls them. The owner then judges a setter or an advert on a number that is wrong.
- **Shaky appointments before they happen.** An appointment nobody confirmed, or one for a person who already missed once, is the one that will not show. A reminder is cheap; an empty slot is not.

**2. Who feels it.** The **ops person** who is told to "clean up the calendar" and has no list. The **setter or closer** who has no idea who to call first. The **business owner** who pays for leads and cannot see what happened to the booked ones. The **agency owner** who has to show a client a show rate and cannot trust it, across 20 or 100 clients.

**3. The number and action that prove it in five seconds.** On the demo company the first thing on the screen is:
**"22 appointments need you. 8 no-shows nobody has followed up, 8 past appointments with no outcome marked and 6 coming up that may not show."** under it one button, **Follow up the 8 no-shows**, and beside it the **true show rate, 60%**, with the line "8 appointments have no outcome yet. Once marked, the rate will land between 53% and 65%." (The old screen said 60% too, but one line of small print said 8 had no outcome; the rate was shown as if it were solid.) With real deals attached the headline also says "The people behind them have $X of open deals" (the demo has $2,840 on those people).

**4. Why would they pay for this rather than the CRM's own calendar reports or a cheaper tool? (said honestly)**
The CRM has a calendar and an appointment report per client account. What it does not give (this is from the product's own description and from F3's earlier research, `docs/features/F3.md`; no live account was tried for this rebuild):
- **One list across every client account** of the past appointments nobody marked and the no-shows nobody called, with who to call first. An agency with 40 clients has no such view.
- **A show rate that ignores the unmarked ones and says so**, with the range it could land in, so a number given to a client is defensible.
- **A queue, not a report.** Reports show what happened; this shows what a person should do now, and one click opens the person in the CRM.
- **Our own record** of status changes ("what SubSync saw change") and the tag dates in the lead panel, which the CRM does not keep.

Honest verdict: **this is a good reason to open SubSync every morning, and it is the strongest of the free-tier screens, but on its own it competes with the CRM's calendar and with cheap reporting tools** (AgencyAnalytics-style at $12 to $15 per client). What makes it sellable is that it is alerts-and-queues, not reporting, and that it shares one counting rule with Today, speed to lead and the audit. The paid reason is the combination: speed to lead, unanswered messages and appointments in one morning list. The one thing that would turn it from "a list" into "a fix" is a **write permission to mark the outcome from SubSync**: one press instead of a trip to the CRM. That needs King's approval and a scope (a proposal, not built).

**5. What to cut (cut in the rebuild).** The seven-status chart as a page-level block (four outcome bars behind one toggle replace it); the four number tiles each with its own comparison text; the "Do these first" tiles that repeated the headline; the page-wide calendar, person and source filters (the full list has search and filters where they are needed); the "By calendar, person and client account" tables with "Booked / Showed / No-show / Show rate: n/a" repeated 30 times (one comparison with tabs, most to do first); the "How to use this page" paragraph (two lines); the always-open target editor (behind one disclosure). Not built on purpose: a booking-source breakdown on this tab (the Insights tab already has show rate by source and needs 3 appointments per source; adding it here would be a second place for the same chart), a dialer, any message sent from SubSync, any write to an appointment.

## Part 2. The old screen, reviewed

Photographed on the demo company at 1440, 768 and 390 for the four roles (agency all accounts, agency inside one account `?as=location`, a client's staff `?as=client`, a business owner `?as=business`): `docs/review/appointments-old-<role>-<width>.png` (role names: `agency-all`, `agency-account`, `client-staff`, `business-owner`). The new screen has the same set: `docs/review/appointments-<role>-<width>.png`.

**What is confusing, repeated or missing (each point seen on the screenshots or in the code):**

1. **The page is 3,600 px tall at 1440 (3,400 px on a phone) and no list is on the first screen.** Above the first useful line there are: the shell's notice, a permission warning, a "How to use this page" box, three filter dropdowns (Person, Calendar, Where the lead came from), a one-line headline and a "What to do" line. The first number is below 800 px. To see a single person to call you press a tile, then a row.
2. **The same facts are shown four times.** "8 past appointments have no outcome recorded" (headline), "Record the outcome 8" (tile), "No outcome" (a column of the account table), and the "Confirmed" bar. "No-shows 23", "No-shows to rebook 8" and the "No-show" bar are three different numbers for nearly the same idea, with three different windows (30 days, 14 days, whatever the dates are).
3. **The first block is four numbers with four comparisons**, one of which reads "50% of 8 against 50% of 10: too few leads to call it a change". It says "leads" about appointments, and it explains a non-result. At 390 px those four tiles take two screens.
4. **There is no work list.** The page counts the work and never lists it. "Do these first" are three tiles you must press, and each opens a table with seven columns (contact, when, calendar, status, assigned to, a link).
5. **A past, unmarked appointment is called a status.** The status chart shows "Confirmed 0" for an account whose unmarked appointments are "booked" in the platform's words. The thing a person must fix (no outcome) is not a bar.
6. **Calendar and person tables are a wall.** "Booked: 4 Showed: 0 No-show: 0 Show rate: n/a" repeated for every calendar of every account (30 lines), and "Team member (name not available)" as a row. Nothing says which line to act on. The account table (agency) is sorted by bookings, not by what needs doing.
7. **Nothing to act with.** No call, no email, no deal value, no "who first". The row link is "Call in your CRM" only when the contact has a phone, and there is no warning when the person asked not to be contacted. There is no tag date or timeline until you open the second panel ("See the lead").
8. **Roles.** A client's staff and a business owner get the same page, including the show-rate target form (a client's staff see it as read-only text) and the "By client account" table is correctly hidden, but the filters, tiles and tables are written for an agency. The page never says "my account".
9. **Phone.** No sideways scroll, but the page is a long single column of small tiles and tables; the number to act on is never at the top; the Person and Calendar dropdowns are full-width rows.
10. **Words.** "UTC days", "Booked: 4 Showed: 0", "recorded outcomes", "rebook", "Remind today" are working words; "Needs approval" appears next to the number it explains only on the agency view.
11. **Scale.** The page loads up to 60,000 appointments in one answer and sends up to 500 rows per list; at 100 accounts the account table sends all 100 and shows 10 with "View all". It worked, but the first read was heavy and the list sent was not paged by the server.

**What the rebuild does about each point:** (1) a headline and one button first, then the lists; (2) one sentence, one count per fact, the three kinds never overlap and add up to the headline; (3) one figure (true show rate) with a plain sentence and an honest range; a change is claimed only from 10 marked outcomes in both periods; (4) three plain lists of five rows each with See all; (5) "no outcome" is its own list and its own bar; (6) one comparison, most to do first, with the count opening exactly those rows; (7) a button per row (Call, or Open), a row menu (Show, Call, Email, Open in your CRM), the person panel with tags and the day each was first seen, the timeline, deals, the do-not-disturb warning before any call; (8) the same screen for every role, scoped on the server; (9) thumb-sized buttons, no sideways scroll (tested at 390); (10) plain words, reviewed with the UX copy skill; (11) the server sends five rows per list and pages the rest.
