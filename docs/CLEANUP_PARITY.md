## 1. The money-first test (plain words)

**Pain.** A client's contact list fills with duplicates, typos, throwaway emails, numbers in ten formats and test junk. Staff text the same person twice, campaigns bounce, reports are inflated, and the client sees the mess. Nobody cleans it because the CRM's own tool works one client account at a time and needs an administrator.

**Who feels it.** The ops person who cleans (hours per client); the setter who calls the wrong copy; the business owner who sees "Ada Lovelace" twice; the agency owner whose reports are wrong in all 40 clients and who cannot see which client is worst.

**The five-second proof number.** "Data quality 87 out of 100 across 13 clients. The worst: Green Leaf Landscaping, 81, 70 problems, 9 duplicate groups." One number, the worst client named, one button.

**Why pay $9 per client account instead of the standard tools.** The standard tools are built for ONE database and priced for one: Insycle from $100 a month per portal (annual billing; each client database needs its own plan through its agency link, V-3P), Dedupely from $40 a month for 30,000 records (V), Cloudingo $2,500 to $10,000 a year (V-3P, weak), DemandTools about $3,000 to $6,000 a year per Salesforce org (V-3P), DataGroomr about $1,500 to $5,800 a year (V-3P). Plauti does not publish a price and is the top price bracket on G2. Only Koalify (free under 10,000 records, then $25 a month, V) and DupeCatcher (free, V) are cheap, and they are narrow (HubSpot-only rules, Salesforce-only prevention). An agency with 20 clients pays SubSync $180 a month in total, finds and fixes across all 20 in one screen, and can show each client a before-and-after score. No other tool in this list has a cross-client console: Insycle has an agency mode but each client is a separate login and plan; Dedupely lets several portals sync into one account but has no agency pricing page (404).

**What to cut / not build.** Cross-object matching (companies, deals): SubSync copies contacts, not companies, so it is not built (proposal). Real-time prevention at record creation: needs a hook inside the CRM; what SubSync can honestly do is the "Stop new duplicates" recipe and the import check. Enrichment (finding missing emails from outside data): needs a data vendor; not built. Phonetic matching beyond last-name Soundex and nicknames: a short list is enough for contacts.

## 3. The real merge: what is possible (VERIFIED on 5 Nov 2026 from the platform's help pages and API description)

- The public API has **no merge call**. `GET /contacts/search/duplicate` only finds one contact for an email or phone.
- The workflow action **Merge Contact** takes **no second contact**: it looks at the contact in the workflow, finds another contact in the same account with the same email, phone or both (the customer chooses which) and merges them. **The older record survives.** What it does with Do Not Disturb, appointments and payments is not written down; the manual merge's article says appointments, payments and invoices keep the kept contact's values.
- An app **can add a contact to a workflow** by API (`POST /contacts/{contactId}/workflow/{workflowId}`, scope `contacts.write`, a location token). An app **cannot create a workflow** (the workflows API has only a list call), so the customer builds one workflow once per client.
- So the safest "one click" is: SubSync prepares the pair, the customer confirms, SubSync adds the newer record to their merge workflow, the CRM merges. It is honest about four limits: it needs an exact email or phone match; it merges ANY other contact sharing that value (so SubSync refuses when a third record shares it); the older record always wins (SubSync says when that differs from its pick); a merge cannot be undone, so Do Not Disturb stops it.
- What is NOT verified: whether the workflow must be published for the API enrolment (SubSync checks the status if the optional scope is present, otherwise it says so in the first-test plan), the `Version` header value (the documents disagree: `2021-07-28` and `v3`), and what the merge does with Do Not Disturb. All three are in the first live test plan.
- Sources: help.gohighlevel.com article "Workflow action: Merge Contact" (155000007464), the changelog entry `highlevel.canny.io/changelog/merge-contact-action`, marketplace.gohighlevel.com/docs/ghl/contacts/add-contact-to-workflow, the public API description `GoHighLevel/highlevel-api-docs` (`apps/contacts.json`, `apps/opportunities.json`, `apps/workflows.json`), the help article "How to Manage and Merge Duplicate Contacts" (48001202210), and the request "Merge Contacts through API" (66 votes, no answer) at `highlevel.canny.io/apis/p/merge-contacts-through-api`.

**What they do well (copied):** a queue that loads the next pair after each decision; one bulk rule with an escape hatch; showing only what differs; a before and after preview; a reason with every suggestion; a recoverable "No".
**What they do badly (beaten):** setup before first value; jargon (master record, survivor, criteria); field-by-field grids as the default; irreversible merges with no preview; no confidence cue.

**Our design (5 Nov redesign).** One question per screen, "Are these the same person?", the two people as two cards with only what differs shown and the identical fields said once underneath. A big visual match meter with one plain reason ("Same email and phone number"). One big "Yes, combine them" beside "No, different people"; "Decide later" and "Skip" are quiet. An all-clean empty state says so. Data quality opens with a score ring and three big cards (Fix phone numbers, Fix emails, Remove junk) each with a real before and after and one button. ## 7. Round 2 (5 Nov): vendor pages King supplied, advanced matching, real-time prevention

**Vendor pages.** The AppExchange listing King gave (appexchange.salesforce.com listingId a0N300000016bXjEAI) is **DemandTools by Validity** (VERIFIED, 4.69 from 442 reviews, paid, no price listed): configurable matching algorithms and filters, merging, duplicate prevention; DataLoader with validation; mass changes by formula; email verification logged on each record; **undo and restore, including undo merge, backed by automatically generated restore files**; notifications to Slack, Teams or email when a scenario runs; usage tracking of every scenario run. datagroomr.com answered 403 and cloudingo.com returned a truncated page, so those rows stay V-3P. What we lack and could cheaply add: notification when a scan runs (we alert only on NEW duplicates, through the bell; email and Slack stay behind their off switches) and a run log (the History tab covers it). Restore files for merges are not possible here: the platform's merge is permanent.

**Advanced matching (built, "Look deeper", on in Thorough).** Weighted evidence instead of one rule: name (exact, order swapped, nickname, spelling, initial), email (same, written differently, similar name before the @ for a business and a personal address, initial plus last name, same company domain), phone (same, other format, same last seven digits, two digits swapped), when and where added (within 10 minutes, within a day, same source), shared tags. Weights combine as independent evidence, penalties subtract (a phone shared by different first names, a shared info@ mailbox, every detail different), and the result is sure (85 and up), likely (60 to 84) or maybe (40 to 59). **Maybe** is shown on its own (the "Maybe the same person" filter), never in the main review. **Learning:** a pair the person said is not the same person is never linked again (any group it would be in); and per client, once there are 5 yes and 5 no decisions, a signal that mostly appears in rejected pairs loses up to 40 percent of its weight and one that mostly appears in accepted pairs gains up to 25 percent. Fixture of 23 hard cases (`src/lib/__tests__/match-evidence.test.ts`): the old Normal rule had precision 0.75 and recall 0.69; the evidence engine has precision 1.00 and recall 1.00 on the same cases (a small, hand-made set: treat it as proof the logic handles these situations, not as a measured rate on real data). Not possible: "same conversation thread or deal" (each belongs to exactly one contact), company name and custom fields (not copied into SubSync). A transitive cluster is built through links at or above the client's line; the cluster still splits where two records clearly differ.

**Real-time prevention, honestly.** The platform sends a contact-created event within seconds and it carries the new contact's name, email and phone; SubSync already receives it. It cannot stop the CRM creating the record (an app has no hook before creation), and cannot reject a form submission. What it does, inside the webhook answer (time-limited to 2.5 seconds, never failing the webhook): compares the newcomer with the client's contacts (by email, last seven digits, last name) and, if likely or sure, (a) remembers it: a "New contacts that may already exist" card on the Duplicates tab ("X may be Y", Open, Not the same) and the existing alert; (b) only if the client is allowed (Settings) AND "also add the tag possible-duplicate" is ticked for it AND the master switch is on, adds that one tag through the write gateway (`contacts.write`, already optional; audited; adds only); (c) the prevention recipe for forms and the account's own duplicate settings (Rules tab). Enrolling the new contact in the customer's workflow would work the same way through the gateway and is left to the workflow owner (the other session). Not possible: blocking at creation, silent auto-merge of arrivals (a merge cannot be undone).
